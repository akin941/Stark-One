# The `rf` file format (version 1)

STARK ONE stores Sub-GHz captures as small text files under `/sd/stark/captures/`
(`rf_NNNN.rf`, TASKS.md STARK-0603, ADR-0022) so they can be read on a PC. Written and
parsed by `components/stark_subghz/subghz_raw.c`. A capture is the demodulated data line
of the radio as timed symbols; it is **our own data**, not a code database (SECURITY_SCOPE
§2): there is no import of third-party sets.

```
stark-subghz 1
freq 433920000
mod ook
rate 4800
symbols 192
400 -1200 1200 -400 400 -1200 ... 1200 -12400
...
```

## Grammar

Lines end in `\n` (a `\r` before it is blank space). Blank lines and `#` comments (from the `#`
to the end of the line, any bytes but NUL) are allowed anywhere, including after the last symbol.
The five header lines come **first, once each, in this order**; then the symbol lines.

| Line | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `stark-subghz 1` | Format and version, nothing else on the line |
| `freq <hz>` | Receive frequency in Hz, **433050000–434790000** (the band lock, ADR-0022 §5) |
| `mod ook` or `mod 2fsk` | The modulation the capture was received in |
| `rate <baud>` | The data rate of the receive profile, 600–50000 (4800 for a capture made by the app) |
| `symbols <n>` | The number of symbols that follow, 1–2048 |
| symbol lines | Signed decimal µs values separated by spaces or tabs, at most 16 to a line |

Numbers are canonical decimal: no `+`, no leading zeros, no hex, no fractions.

## Symbols

* **Sign is level**: positive is the data line high (carrier on), negative low.
* They **alternate**, and the first is positive.
* Each has **1 ≤ |v| ≤ 1 000 000** µs.
* The **sum of |v| is at most 2 000 000 µs** (2 s).
* There are exactly `symbols` of them and **nothing after the last** but blanks and comments.

## What a capture keeps

Between Start and Stop the service keeps what the data line produced, with these rules
(`subghz_cap_feed()`): leading silence (low before the first high) is dropped; adjacent
symbols of the same level are merged; a symbol is cut to 1 s; the capture ends at 2048 symbols
or when the kept symbols span 2 s (the last one is cut to fit, so a signal of 3 s keeps its first
2 s). The 2 s is the span of the kept signal, not wall-clock time: waiting for a remote costs
nothing.

## Parsing is strict

The parser accepts exactly the grammar above and answers `STARK_ERR_INVALID_ARG` to everything
else — a missing or repeated header line, a header line out of order, an unknown key, a
frequency outside the band, a wrong sign order, a zero, a value over 1 s, a total over 2 s, more
symbols than declared, fewer than declared (a truncated file), anything after the last symbol,
a line over 160 characters (before any `#`), a NUL or other control byte, a file over 24 576
bytes. It reads a file in pieces of any size through a 160-byte line buffer, never reads past
what it was given and never writes past the symbol buffer it was given; a file that does not
load leaves nothing behind (the app shows it as an error and does not open it). The firmware
writes a file only if it would read back identically.

A file is at most 19 kB (2048 symbols of at most 8 characters and a space, plus 128 line ends
and the header), so it is streamed through a 256-byte chunk both ways and no whole-file buffer
exists.

## Names

`rf_NNNN.rf`, `NNNN` the next number above the highest on the card (0001–9999); the Saved list
shows the newest first. A file saved by hand under another name is not listed.

## Timing histogram

The app and the log show the durations grouped by ±20 %: the shortest duration not yet grouped,
`lo`, starts a bin holding every duration up to 1.5 × `lo` (a window of ±20 % about 1.25 × `lo`);
at most 8 bins, ascending, each with its count and mean (rounded to the nearest µs), and what is
longer than the eighth bin counted as "Longer". The grouping does not depend on the order of the
symbols. The decoders of STARK-0604 use the first group of at least two members as the shortest
cluster and do their own two-cluster split (docs/subghz-decode.md).

## Test scenes

`test/fake/fake_subghz_scenes.h` (generated by `scripts/gen_subghz_vectors.py`) holds the scenes
the test-only fake CC1101 plays on GDO2: pulse-width, pulse-distance and Manchester signals with
round-number timings, and two limit scenes. They are **spec-derived, never recordings of a real
device**, and each carries the expectation a capture of it must meet (symbols kept, fingerprint,
histogram), computed by code written independently of the firmware. Captures of our own remote
join at V1 (docs/bringup/v1-subghz.md). The decoder vectors (STARK-0604) live in
`test/fake/fake_subghz_decode_vectors.h`, host tests only (docs/subghz-decode.md).
